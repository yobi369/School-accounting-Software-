# ShuleLedger licensing

**Current status: proprietary; all rights reserved.**

ShuleLedger is being developed as a product for real Kenyan schools, with the option to provide paid deployments, support, hosting, and commercial licenses. The repository is not released under an OSI-approved open-source license.

## What this means

- Public visibility of source code is not permission to reuse it.
- No permission is granted to copy, modify, redistribute, sublicense, host as a service, or sell ShuleLedger without prior written authorization.
- A school or organization must obtain a separate written agreement before production or commercial use is authorized.
- Access to a test deployment, if one is offered later, will not by itself grant rights to the source code.
- The root [LICENSE](../LICENSE) contains the current proprietary notice.

## Third-party software

ShuleLedger uses third-party packages and other materials. Each dependency remains subject to its own license. This project's proprietary notice does not override third-party licenses or claim ownership of their code. Before a commercial release, generate and review a dependency license inventory and confirm that every dependency's terms are compatible with the distribution model.

## Commercial licensing policy

Commercial terms should be agreed in writing before onboarding a paying school. The agreement should clearly identify:

- the licensed school or legal entity and permitted users;
- whether the license covers self-hosting, hosted access, or both;
- fees, term, renewal, and termination;
- support, maintenance, updates, backups, service availability, and recovery responsibilities;
- data ownership, confidentiality, security incident handling, export, retention, and deletion;
- permitted integrations, limits on redistribution, and treatment of custom development;
- applicable law, dispute resolution, and liability allocation.

Do not treat this checklist as a signed contract or as an offer with fixed pricing. Have a qualified Kenyan lawyer review the final customer agreement, privacy terms, and data-processing terms before handling real school records.

## Future licensing changes

Keep the project proprietary unless the copyright holder deliberately chooses another strategy. Switching later to an open-source or dual-licensing model should happen only after reviewing contributor ownership/permissions, third-party license compatibility, and the business impact. Do not advertise the project as open source while this proprietary notice is in force.
